## Verdict: **FAIL — 2 BLOCKER, 6 MAJOR, 9 MINOR.**

**The diagnosis is exact, the cost analysis is the most honest in this design's history, and the specification of the remedy contradicts itself about which Agents it covers and what it does to `GovernanceSkipped`.** I attacked the cost claim hardest, because the brief said three amendments here have been re-drafted over mis-stated read costs; it survives, clause by clause, and I could not break it. What does not survive is the rule. Two normative statements in A80 give opposite answers for the same Agent, and the gate A80 chose excludes the one state it names three times as covered. Neither is a hard problem — each fix is a sentence and, in one case, a test row — but an implementer cannot build from the text as it stands, and the three choices go to the human resting on a residual-risk claim that is understated by about half.

### What I verified, by trying to break it

- **The defect is real, and every code citation but one is exact.** `routeConverged` has exactly four call sites (`authtxn.go:1164`, `:1328`, `:1717`, `:1811`) and all four are inside a stage arm of `Create` or `Lock`; the function is at `:2518` and checks `ObservedGeneration != rt.Generation` as claimed. `reconcileServed` is at `:1498`; on the API-key path it reads the route with `currentServingRoute` (`httproute.go:318`, a plain `r.Get`) and asks it exactly `routePublished` (`httproute.go:355`, "any rule carries a `backendRef`"), and touches `status.parents` nowhere. `Ready=True` is set at `agent_controller.go:697`, `:756`, `:780`, `:806`, and `withholdReady` (`:944`, called at `:878`, `:884`, `:893`) is the only thing that takes it back, driven by `gatewayOutcome.withhold`, which no served path sets for this cause. `GatewayListenerName` is the constant `"http"` at `httproute.go:53` and `servingRouteFor` at `:133` re-asserts it, so the state does not heal. The comment block at `httproute.go:43`–`:52` states the blindness correctly today and is, as A80 says, not the thing that is wrong.
- **"Nothing else notices" is exhaustive for the states it examines, and I could not add to it.** `freshNack` is called only at `authtxn.go:1150` and `:1705`, both inside a stage; `GenuineNack` (`authnack.go:53`) requires the Gateway as `involvedObject` and agentgateway's controller as source. `foreignTrafficPolicies` (`authforeign.go:37`) lists policies and reads no route status. `reportAboveServed` (`authtxn.go:354`) reads the Gateway and the policies in its namespace and returns early on `auth.Mode == "" || auth.Transaction != nil`, so it never touches the Agent's route or the Agent's own policy. The card fetch (`card.go:163`–`:191`) addresses the revision's ClusterIP and deliberately bypasses the gateway, so `Registered=True` keeps being written throughout. I looked for a fifth watcher and found none.
- **The cost claim holds on every clause.** (1) The route really is cached: `cmd/operator/main.go:154`–`:155` disables the client cache only for `ConfigMap`, `Secret` and `Namespace`, and `SetupWithManager` registers `Watches(&gatewayv1.HTTPRoute{}, byAgentLabels)` at `agent_controller.go:1884` under `r.Gateway.Enabled`, so the informer holds the object and its status. (2) There is **no** generation predicate anywhere on that builder — no `WithEventFilter`, no `builder.WithPredicates` on the route watch — so the Gateway's own status write does enqueue the Agent. (3) The policy really is read live twice per served API-key pass, at `authtxn.go:2231` (`authPolicyPresent`, which discards the object) and `:2254` (`reassertServedPolicy`, which keeps it), and controller-runtime does not cache unstructured objects. (4) `reassertServedPolicy` really does hold the whole object with `status.ancestors` in it, and `writeAuthPolicy` (`:2452`) short-circuits on a digest match, so the steady-state pass makes no write — which is what makes case 19 (f)'s `resourceVersion` assertion buildable. (5) `compiler.Digest` (`internal/compiler/auth.go:410`) projects apiVersion, kind, name, namespace, three labels and `spec` only, so a test writing `status.ancestors` does not perturb the digest and does not provoke a rewrite. (6) The 5-minute backstop is real: `CardDriftInterval` at `card.go:336`, `cardRequeueAfter` at `:377`, returned through `earliest` at `agent_controller.go:907`. **No new object, watch, read or requeue is needed. That claim is true.**
- **The two halves really do fail in opposite directions, and two reasons rather than one is right.** `policyConverged` (`:2554`) exists and is reachable only from a stage; `reconcileServed` checks presence and re-renders, never `status.ancestors`. §3.3.2's record of the upstream fixture is accurate as quoted (`03:648`: `Accepted=True, reason: Valid` beside `Attached=False`, `output: []`, `http-route-missing-not-attached.yaml`). Splitting fail-closed from fail-open, and grounding the split in §5's own "a bare condition with three causes and one message is rule 8's loud-and-wrong", is the right call.
- **Case 19 is buildable, and the harness already has every helper it needs.** `test/envtest/authcreate_test.go:270` `acceptRoute` writes a `status.parents` entry for `assayd/assayd-gateway` at `rt.Generation`; `:298` `acceptPolicy` writes the Gateway ancestor with `Accepted=Valid`/`Attached` at the policy's generation through `k8s.Status().Update`, so the CRD has a status subresource and (f) is writable. `authgate_test.go` already carries `mintRevision(carded)`, `backendOf`, `servingRoute` and — decisively for (c) — `acceptedGen`, whose own comment reads "A sub-case holds the Gateway's reported generation at its pre-move value simply by not accepting the route again." That is (c)'s whole mechanism, already in the suite.
- **(c) is the best row in the amendment, and its mutation is real.** `routeConverged` returns false on `c.ObservedGeneration != rt.Generation`, so substituting it for "not reported broken at the current generation" makes the Agent `Degraded` on the pass after a promotion. It compiles (same signature, same scope), it is exactly option (A3), and it kills (c). (d) is its twin and pins the admission rather than the fix. A recommended call defended by a test row that fails under the obvious alternative is how this should be done.
- **Appending to `incompleteOrder` moves nothing.** The list is `[AuthPolicyMissing, ForeignTrafficPolicy, GatewayAuthPolicy]` (`authtxn.go:487`) and `raiseIncomplete` (`:500`) replaces the standing reason only when the new one outranks it; appending at ranks 3 and 4 can never outrank an existing entry, and `incompleteRank` returns `-1` for unlisted reasons, which the `default` arm already handles. - **The `Adopt` and `auth: none` paths do hold the route object already.** `ensureServingRoute` (`httproute.go:249`) returns the stored route, and its return is discarded at `authtxn.go:1525` (served) and `:2288` (`refuseAdopt`). On the steady-state pass it is `&existing` from the cached `Get`, status included.
- **The honesty is carried everywhere it should be**: "Owed, not built" in §1.1's new row, §1.1's conditions row, §3.1, §3.3.3, both §5 rows, §8.1's lead sentence and case 19 itself; "**not yet critiqued**" in the README row; and "nothing in it is measured beyond the k3d observation" in the Status line. The two provenance bullets that named this defect on 2026-09-12 are marked rather than rewritten, which is the right treatment.

## BLOCKER findings

### BLOCKER 1 — the empty-slot gate provably excludes the refused `Adopt`, which A80 names as covered three times

`03:820` (§3.3.3), `03:1339` (§5's route row), `03:1477` (§11's cost bullet).

§3.3.3 gates the re-derivation on "a pass of a served Agent with **no** transaction in the slot — a transaction has its own stage checks and its own reasons, and must not be second-guessed here". §5's route row repeats the trigger ("with no transaction in the slot") and then, in the same cell, says "It covers a served `apikey` Agent, a served `auth: none` Agent and a refused `Adopt`."

A refused `Adopt` always has a transaction in the slot, permanently and by design. `refuseAdopt` writes `status.Auth = &AuthStatus{Transaction: &AuthTransaction{Kind: TxAdopt, Stage: StageRefused, RefusedMode: refused}}` (`internal/controller/authtxn.go:2308`–`:2309`), with `Mode` left `""`; ADR-0034 Amendment 3 is why the record is permanent — it is what stops a deleted route becoming a `Create`. `authStep` branches on it first (`:627`–`:631`) and routes to `refuseAdopt`, so `reconcileServed` — which A80 names as the host of the re-derivation — is never reached for it: the served arm is `case recorded != ""` at `:719`, and a refused `Adopt` fails it twice over.

This is not a cosmetic overclaim, and it is the reverse of harmless. The stated justification for the gate — "a transaction has its own stage checks and its own reasons" — is *false* for this one transaction: `refuseAdopt` runs no stage, calls no `routeConverged`, sets no withhold, and returns `gatewayOutcome{}`. A75's read skips it too (`reportAboveServed` returns on `auth.Mode == ""`). So the refused `Adopt` is the state with the **least** existing coverage, it reports `Ready=True` on a refused route exactly as A80 says, and the gate as written is the one thing that would keep it uncovered. Case 19 cannot catch the gap: its preamble says "Every sub-case is an Agent driven to `Served` under `apikey`", and (e) is the only variant, `auth: none`.

**Fix.** Change the gate at `:820` to "a pass of a served Agent with no transaction in the slot, **or with a refused `Adopt` in it** — a refused `Adopt` runs no stage and checks nothing, which is why it is the one transaction record this judgement is not second-guessing", and carry the same words into `:1339`. Then add case 19 (h): a refused `Adopt` whose route is written `Accepted=False` at the current generation reads `PolicyApplyIncomplete=ServingRouteNotAccepted`, `Ready=False`, phase `Degraded`, with `GovernanceSkipped` still `True`/`CompilerUpgradeUnsupported` and its message intact; mutation — gate the re-derivation on an empty slot, which compiles and must fail it. If instead the human's answer is that `Adopt` stays out of scope, then strike it from `:820` and `:1339` and add it to "What the fix does NOT cover" at `:1483`, naming that A75's read skips it too so nothing reports it at all.

### BLOCKER 2 — §3.1's narrowed predicate and §3.3.3's route half say opposite things about `GovernanceSkipped`, and §3.1's gate is wider than §3.3.3's

`03:256` (§3.1's clearing rule) against `03:820` (§3.3.3) and `03:1339` (§5).

§3.1, as A80 narrows it: "After `Served`, the predicate is the weaker one A80 narrows it to: not that the tuple is reported holding right now, but that **neither the route's tuple nor the policy's** has been reported BROKEN". Read as the predicate for `GovernanceSkipped=False`, a route reported `Accepted=False` makes it fail, so `GovernanceSkipped` must go `True`.

§3.3.3 and §5's route row say the opposite, in terms: "`GovernanceSkipped` **keeps the value the served policy earned** and its message gains a note saying the Gateway is not accepting the route, because nothing is reaching this Agent through the gateway and the record of which tier it runs in must not be erased." For a served API-key Agent that value is `False`. A80 is consistent with §3.3.3 everywhere else — only `AuthPolicyNotAttached` is made a `GovernanceSkipped` reason (`03:57`), `ServingRouteNotAccepted` is not, and §3.1 gains no reason that could carry a route refusal — so the §3.1 sentence is the outlier. An implementer reading §3.1 writes a predicate that flips `GovernanceSkipped=True` with no reason to put on it; an implementer reading §3.3.3 writes the opposite; both cite A80. Case 19 (e) follows §3.3.3 and would fail the §3.1 reading.

Compounding it, the two sections use **different gates for the same narrowing**. §3.1 says "After `Served`", unqualified; §3.3.3 says "with no transaction in the slot". A served Agent inside a missing-policy `Lock`, a J2 `Lock` or a route re-create satisfies the first and not the second — and on exactly those paths A77's route gate at `ProbingAfter` deliberately uses the **strong** `routeConverged` (`authtxn.go:1811`). §3.1 as written weakens the predicate there too, which A80's own Status line promises it does not do ("A75's, A77's and A78's rules stand").

**Fix.** At `:256`, narrow the clause to the policy tuple alone — "that the **policy's** tuple has not been reported broken" — and add one sentence: "A route reported broken raises `PolicyApplyIncomplete=ServingRouteNotAccepted` and withholds `Ready` **without moving `GovernanceSkipped`**, whose value records which tier the Agent runs in and must not be erased by an availability failure (§3.3.3)." Replace "After `Served`" with §3.3.3's gate verbatim, and say that a `Create` or `Lock` in flight keeps the strong `routeConverged` tuple at its stages, unchanged by A80.

## MAJOR findings

### MAJOR 3 — case 19 (g) asserts the outcome its own rule forbids, and neither half of its mutation can fail it

`03:1417`.

> **(g) A transaction in the slot is left alone.** … `PolicyApplyIncomplete`'s reason stays `AuthPolicyMissing`, first in `incompleteOrder`, and `ServingRouteNotAccepted` **appears in the message** and not as the reason. Mutation: run the re-derivation whatever the slot holds, **and** put the new reasons ahead of `AuthPolicyMissing` in `incompleteOrder`.

Three things, compounding.

1. **The assertion contradicts the rule.** §3.3.3 says `ServingRouteNotAccepted` "can never stand beside a transaction's reason, because it is raised only with an empty slot". If the slot is left alone, the reason is never produced, so it cannot appear in the message. (g)'s pass condition is reachable only if the re-derivation *did* run.
2. **The first half of the mutation produces the asserted outcome rather than breaking it.** Were the re-derivation to run regardless of the slot and reach `raiseIncomplete` (`authtxn.go:500`), `ServingRouteNotAccepted` at rank 4 against a standing `AuthPolicyMissing` at rank 0 takes the `default` arm: reason kept, `" | ServingRouteNotAccepted: …"` appended to the message. That is (g)'s stated pass condition, verbatim.
3. **`incompleteOrder` is not the writer on that path at all.** The missing-policy `Lock` sets both conditions with `conds.set` directly (`authtxn.go:1905` and `:1911`), not `raiseIncomplete`. So the second half of the mutation — reordering `incompleteOrder` — changes nothing for (g) either. Both halves are inert, and a compound mutation whose halves are individually inert proves nothing about either.

There is a fourth problem underneath: A77's route gate at `ProbingAfter` already runs `routeConverged` and folds its failure into `unmet`, which the missing-policy message carries (`:1903`). So "the route's refusal is named in the message while the reason stays `AuthPolicyMissing`" is already true at `origin/main`, with no A80 in the build.

**Fix.** Restate (g) to assert an **absence**: "`PolicyApplyIncomplete`'s reason is `AuthPolicyMissing`, and neither `ServingRouteNotAccepted` nor `AuthPolicyNotAttached` appears in any of the Agent's conditions." Make the mutation the single edit "run the re-derivation whatever the slot holds", which then makes one of the two appear and kills the row. Drop the `incompleteOrder` clause from (g) — or move it to a row where `raiseIncomplete` is actually the writer, such as a served Agent with a foreign `traffic` policy beside a refused route, which is the only state in which the appended ranks are exercised at all.

### MAJOR 4 — the two new conditions are not in A75's carry set, so they clear on any pass that returns before the `-auth` step, and the page A80 rests on never accumulates duration

`03:822` ("Both reasons page, through design 10's alert on `PolicyApplyIncomplete` by type and duration, **which is what makes this a report rather than a log line**").

`PolicyApplyIncomplete` is owned and not sticky, so a pass that does not assert it clears it — A80 says so itself, approvingly, when it costs clearing. A75 hit exactly this and built machinery for it: `seedStoredAbove` (`authtxn.go:414`), called from `agent_controller.go:387` before anything can return early, carries the last pass's conditions forward with their `LastTransitionTime` intact, and `carriedReason` (`:464`) names what it carries:

```go
func carriedReason(reason string) bool {
	return reason == ReasonGatewayAuthPolicy || reason == ReasonForeignTrafficPolicy
}
```

`AuthPolicyNotAttached` and `ServingRouteNotAccepted` are in neither that function nor `seedStoredAbove`'s two explicit blocks. There are several returns after `:387` that write status and never reach the `-auth` step — the run-namespace error paths (`agent_controller.go:412`, `:416`), `reportUnresolvablePin` (`:432`) and `reportCollision` (`:462`). On any such pass both new conditions vanish and their `LastTransitionTime` resets on the next pass that re-raises them. Design 10's rule is "`PolicyApplyIncomplete` open >5m (page)"; a condition that resets does not stay open. The remedy's stated mechanism — "it pages" — is therefore not established, and it is the clause that makes A80 a report rather than a log line.

**Fix.** In §3.3.3's cost paragraph, add: "Both reasons join A75's carry set (`seedStoredAbove`, `carriedReason`), for its reason: a pass that returns before the `-auth` step reads neither object, and clearing a report nothing re-read would reset the duration design 10 alerts on." Add a case 19 row: raise the policy half, then force a pass that returns before the `-auth` step, and assert both conditions survive with their `LastTransitionTime` unchanged; mutation — leave the reasons out of the carry set, which compiles and must fail it.

### MAJOR 5 — choice A's residual risk is understated: A75's read covers a Gateway that cannot be READ, not one that has stopped REPORTING

`03:825` (§3.3.3's admission) and `03:1475` (choice A's cost cell, on the **recommended** option).

Both say the same thing: "(A1) … **misses a Gateway that stops reporting at all**, which A75's read covers for API-key Agents and nothing covers for `auth: none`."

A75's read covers a different failure. `gatewayAuthPolicies` (`authabove.go:199`) sets `out.unreadable` only when the `Get` of the Gateway returns an error — `NoMatch`, `NotFound`, `Forbidden` or transient (`:205`–`:216`) — or when the policy list fails (`:224`). A Gateway whose agentgateway controller has stopped writing status is read perfectly: `unreadable` is empty, `listeners` is empty, no policy counts, `stands()` (`:101`) is false, and `reportAboveServed` returns having reported nothing at all.

So under the recommended (A1), a Gateway that is present and readable but no longer reporting is detected by **nothing, for every mode** — API-key Agents included — not just for `auth: none`. That is the exact failure mode of a crashed or wedged gateway controller, and it is the one thing (A1) gives up. The human is being asked to accept a residual risk that is roughly twice what the table states, on the option the amendment recommends.

**Fix.** Rewrite both sentences: "A75's read reports a Gateway the operator **cannot read**; a Gateway that is readable but whose controller has stopped writing route and policy status is a different failure and is detected by nothing here, for any mode." Then restate what (A2) buys against the corrected baseline — which is the whole of that miss, not half of it.

### MAJOR 6 — choice A's option set omits the variant that closes the miss with no CRD change, and (A2)'s stated cost is not required

`03:1475`.

> (A2) catches that, and **needs a timestamp in status**: `status.auth` is the only home, `authKept` guards its shape, and the CRD ships under `crds/`, which `helm upgrade` never updates — and it pages on a slow Gateway.

A CRD field is not required, and the hazard named — the one this project has been burned by, and the most discouraging item in the cell — is avoidable two ways. (i) The clock is already on the objects: the assayd Gateway's own `status.parents[].conditions[].lastTransitionTime` on the route (and the equivalent on the policy's ancestor) says when the Gateway last reported anything, and the route's `creationTimestamp` covers the no-entry case. "Last report older than the window" is computable from what the pass already holds — the same objects A80's own cost argument is built on. (ii) A third option the table does not list at all: report the unknown state as a condition **immediately**, with no in-operator window, and let design 10's duration threshold be the grace period — which is precisely the mechanism A80 relies on for its own two pages at `:822`. That variant needs no new field, no new read and no CRD change; its real cost is condition churn on every promotion, which is a different and much smaller cost than the one stated, and it need not withhold `Ready`.

This matters because the cell is the human's decision surface. (A2) is presented as expensive and CRD-shaped, so (A1)'s named miss reads as unavoidable. It is not.

**Fix.** Add the third variant to the options cell — "**(A2′)** report unknown at once as its own reason and let design 10's duration threshold be the grace window" — and correct (A2)'s cost to name the two no-new-field clocks, leaving its true costs (a window to tune, and a page on a slow Gateway; for A2′, status churn on every promotion). Recheck the ADR claim afterwards: (A2)'s place in "reverses a safety trade this slice records" rests partly on the CRD hazard, and (A2′) reverses nothing.

### MAJOR 7 — the policy half's precondition is not what any existing read computes, and taking "the object `reassertServedPolicy` already holds" at face value would misreport a foreign policy

`03:823` (§3.3.3), `03:1340` (§5), `03:1479` (§11's cost bullet: "`reassertServedPolicy` already holds the whole object, `status.ancestors` included. **No new read.**").

A80's precondition is "a policy that is **present and digests to `status.auth.appliedDigest`**". `reassertServedPolicy` (`authtxn.go:2243`) computes no such quantity. It renders a fresh policy from the Agent, digests **the render**, and compares that to `appliedDigest` (`:2268`); it never digests the stored object. The two differ exactly when the operator build changed, which is the I1 case the design cares about.

Worse, the function has two early returns that leave `existing` populated with something the caller must not judge:

- `IsNotFound` → `return nil` at `:2257`, with no object at all;
- `existing.GetLabels()[LabelAgentUID] != string(agent.UID)` → `return nil` at `:2266`, with `existing` holding a policy that **is not this Agent's**, a state §3.2 rules is `ForeignTrafficPolicy` and which the operator must neither take over nor judge.

An implementer following `:1479` literally would read that foreign object's `status.ancestors` and raise `AuthPolicyNotAttached` against this Agent — rule 8's plausible cause that was never checked, in the amendment written to eliminate one. `authPolicyPresent` (`:2226`) cannot supply the precondition either: it checks neither digest nor UID and discards the object.

**Fix.** State the precondition as the code can compute it: "an `<agent>-auth` that exists, **carries this Agent's UID label**, and whose rendered form digests to `status.auth.appliedDigest`" — and say the object judged is the one `reassertServedPolicy` read *after* both guards passed, so the function returns it rather than discarding it. Add a case 19 row: a policy at the `-auth` name without this Agent's UID, with `Attached=False`, must read `ForeignTrafficPolicy` and **not** `AuthPolicyNotAttached`; mutation — judge the object before the UID guard, which compiles and must fail it.

### MAJOR 8 — where the judgement runs is unspecified, and the plausible placements differ in outcome

`03:820` ("the operator judges two objects it has already read in that pass"), `03:823` (the ordering argument), `03:1477`.

A80 never says where in `reconcileGateway` the judgement sits, and it is not a detail:

- placed **inside or before** the `-auth` step, its `PolicyApplyIncomplete` is destroyed by the transaction paths and by the served paths, which use `conds.set` directly and overwrite reason *and* message (`authtxn.go:1905`, `:1911`, `:1917`);
- placed **after** `authStep`, beside `reportForeign` (`authtxn.go:325`) and `reportAboveServed` (`:330`), it merges through `raiseIncomplete` and honours `incompleteOrder`.

A80's whole ordering argument at `:823` — "appending leaves what those two report unchanged" — is true only under the second placement, and the `Ready=False`/`Degraded` outcome it asserts happens only if the judgement calls `withholdInOrder` (`:516`), which A80 never names even though its own §11 bullet correctly identifies `withholdReady`/`gatewayOutcome.withhold` as the only mechanism that takes `Ready` back.

A second, smaller ambiguity of the same kind: **which** route object is judged. On the API-key path `currentServingRoute` reads the route before `ensureServingRoute` may write it; the two differ in generation on a pass that promotes, and therefore differ in whether a standing refusal is "at the current generation" or a generation behind. Name one.

**Fix.** In §3.3.3's cost paragraph, state the placement: "after the `-auth` step, beside `reportForeign` and `reportAboveServed`, through `raiseIncomplete` and `withholdInOrder`, because the transaction and served paths write `PolicyApplyIncomplete` with `conds.set` directly and would overwrite it." And add: "the route judged is the one read at the top of the pass, before any write of this pass, so a promotion's own generation bump reads as unknown on the pass that makes it."

## MINOR findings

1. **The RBAC marker is cited at the wrong line.** `03:1479` says "the RBAC marker at `agent_controller.go:1867` says so in those words". ## On the framing put to the human

**The shape is right, and two of the three cells are not yet fit to decide on.**

What is right: the choice is framed as *how*, never *whether* — "The choice is not *whether* to fix it" is the correct opening for a rule-8 defect. What is not fit yet:

MAJOR 6: the option set omits the variant that closes that miss with no CRD change, and (A2)'s stated cost — a timestamp in `status.auth`, under `crds/`, which `helm upgrade` never updates — is not required.
