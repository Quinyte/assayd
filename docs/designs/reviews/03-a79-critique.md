## Verdict: **PASS — 0 BLOCKER, 0 MAJOR, 8 MINOR.**

**The diagnosis is exact, the correction is right, sub-case (i) is buildable as written, and the Option A call is sound.** I tried to break each of the amendment's load-bearing claims and could not. What is left is eight one-sentence findings, none of which touches a rule, a decision, a condition, a message or the amendment's conclusion, and none of which would mislead an implementer past the sentence it sits in.

### What I verified, by trying to break it

- **The trace is right, line by line.** In `runLock`'s combined `Converging`/`ProbingAfter` arm the order is: `authPolicyIntact` → `freshNack` → (`Converging` only) the §3.3.2 tuple → A75's `gatewayAuthPolicies` read → the `ProbingAfter` foreign check on `out.foreign` → the re-point (`reCreate && cardedRevision`) → the route gate (`routeConverged`) → the probe. A79 states exactly that order. A failed intact read sets the stage to `ApplyingPolicies`, persists and `continue`s, and the next iteration breaks at `foreignAtOwnName`. So nothing below the intact read runs.
- **(c) cannot reach the ordering (h) credited it with, and the reason is the digest.** Case 18 (c) relabels the `Lock`'s own `<agent>-auth` with another Agent's UID. `compiler.Digest` projects `LabelAgentUID` into the canonical form, so the relabel changes the digest, `authPolicyIntact` returns not-intact at its **first** read, and the pass rewinds and breaks at `foreignAtOwnName`. `TestNoBackendMovesWhileThePolicyIsNotThisAgents` asserts `ApplyingPolicies`, which is the proof the arm was left at its top. A79's "(c) pins the re-point against the stage loop, not against the foreign check" is correct, and so is its gloss that (c) is **not weakened** — only credited with one ordering too many.
- **The correction to the brief is right: `foreignTrafficPolicies` does not require another Agent's UID.** Its skip is a conjunction — `p.GetName() == own` **and** `p.GetLabels()[LabelAgentUID] == string(agent.UID)`. A policy under any other name fails the first conjunct and is reported whatever UID label it carries, or none. `plantForeign` sets `SetLabels(nil)` and is therefore usable verbatim.
- **(i)'s assertions are right on every clause I could check, including the two subtle ones.** (1) `PolicyApplyIncomplete` reads reason `AuthPolicyMissing` even though `reportForeign` runs after `runLock`: `incompleteOrder` is `[AuthPolicyMissing, ForeignTrafficPolicy, GatewayAuthPolicy]`, so `raiseIncomplete(ForeignTrafficPolicy)` takes the `default` branch and appends rather than replacing. (2) `missingPolicyRouteNote` **names no exits**, because `cardedRevision(status, status.activeRevision)` is true for a carded r2 and the function returns after its first clause — the corrected assertion A79 says it caught in its own first draft is the right one, and the wrong one it replaced would indeed have asserted a message the operator does not emit in that state. With `repointed` false the clause reads "left as found", and with the mutation applied it reads "re-pointed at status.activeRevision r2" and the `backendRef` assertion fails too, so the row has two independent kills.
- **(i) is buildable, and the build A79 specifies reaches the state.** `missingPolicyLock` leaves a re-creation `Lock` at `ProbingAfter`; the re-creation branch of `runLock` never calls `ensureServingRoute` (it takes `currentServingRoute` and assigns `rt = cur`), so promotion alone does not move the route — which is what (c) already measures. With a transaction in the slot `authStep` routes straight to `runLock`, so a foreign policy under its own name diverts nothing. The forked critic built the row from A79's text alone: it passed on shipped code and failed on the named mutation at the `backendRef` assertion, with r2 behind the route. That is the strongest evidence a row is specified well enough to be built by someone who did not write it.
- **The mutation compiles, and the survivals hold.** "Move the re-point above the foreign check, route gate left under it" is a block move inside one arm, all identifiers in scope. (a), (b), (d)–(h) plant no route-level foreign policy, so `out.foreign` is empty and the two orders are indistinguishable; (c) rewinds above both. (h) and the J2/K2 cases are further insulated because the re-point is guarded by `reCreate`. I also checked the one non-case-18 test that puts a foreign policy beside a `Lock` at `ProbingAfter` — `authlockreview_test.go`'s `TestALockEvaluatesNoAnswerWhileAForeignPolicyStands` — and it is a **J2** `Lock`, `reCreate` false, so the re-point block is a no-op there. So A79's "pinned by nothing in `make test`" is right by construction, and the forked critic's full-suite run corroborates it.
- **Option A is sound, on all three of its reasons.** Design 16's row needs its eval controller: there is no `EvalSuite` type in `api/v1alpha1/`, no eval controller in `internal/controller/`, and `7ad39bf` (PR #40) is a **docs-only** commit — so pinning design 03's shipped ordering behind design 16's row would put it behind another design's unbuilt slice. The ordering lives in `internal/controller/authtxn.go`, which is design 03's. And nothing in `make test` reaches it today. The cost is stated rather than hidden, in both the places that matter.
- **The ADR call is right.** `docs/decisions/0034-…:129` reads "with design 03 §8.1 case 18 (a)–(h) in `test/envtest/authgate_test.go`" — a statement of what PR #48 built, not a completeness claim; and `:126`'s enumeration of case 18's shape ("the two sub-cases that pin the shipped J2 and K2 defect, the one that pins the digest condition, and the one that pins the gate against A75's Gateway read") never credits (c) with the foreign-check ordering. Adding an owed coverage row reverses no decision the amendment records. No ADR edit is owed.
- **The class grep holds: exactly two sites in editable normative text carried the wrong claim**, and both are handled — `03:1389` (corrected in place) and `03:1467`, A77's "Tests owed" bullet (marked as provenance rather than rewritten, which is the right treatment for a provenance record). The other occurrences are in `docs/designs/reviews/`, which is immutable, and in design 16, where the claim is reported **correctly**. I swept §8.1's other pin claims — (b)'s "pins A77's own answer to the first critique's MAJOR 3", (d)'s "pins the gate against the re-point", (h)'s "pins the gate against A75's read" — and each is supported by §11's own mutation table. I found no other sub-case credited with an ordering it does not reach.
- **The honesty about what changed is carried in all four places**: the Status line ("Case 18 (a)–(h) stay built; case 18 (i) is added and owed"), §1.1's slice table row at `:49` ("Case 18 (i) is owed and not built"), §8.1 case 18's header at `:1381` ("(a)–(h) are built … (i) is owed and not built"), and the design 03 README row ("the one unbuilt row in a case that was fully built"). §1.1 is the approval boundary, so the slice table is the right place for it.
- **The asymmetry A79 records is true.** J2's and K2's `ensureServingRoute` write sits in `runLock`'s `else` branch, **above** the `steps` loop and therefore above `foreignAtOwnName`, above A75's read, above the `ProbingAfter` foreign check and above the gate. So under a foreign `traffic` policy a J2 or K2 `Lock`'s route does follow a promotion, and only a missing-policy `Lock` can measure the narrow ordering. See MINOR 1 for where that belongs.

## MINOR findings

1. **`03:775`'s "and its route does not move" reads as a general guarantee and is false for a J2 or K2 pass — and A79 establishes the truth in §11 without putting it in the body.** The sentence is "A pass held earlier — by a foreign policy at the Agent's own name, by a NACK, or by a foreign `traffic` policy on the route — never reaches this step, and its route does not move." The first clause is true of every `Lock`; the second is true only of a missing-policy `Lock`, because J2's and K2's route write has already happened at the top of `runLock` before any of those three holds can fire. The step's heading scopes it to the missing-policy `Lock`, which is the only thing that saves the sentence, and a reader who lands here from the deadline table or from §1.1's row will not re-read the heading. This is rule 7: it states a bound wider than the code enforces, and A79's own justification for (i) — "the guarantee is the missing-policy `Lock`'s alone" — is exactly what `:775` is missing. It is A77's sentence, not A79's, but A79 is the amendment that diagnosed it and is the cheapest place to fix it. This is the finding I would most want taken. **Fix.** At `:775`, after "and its route does not move", add: "— of this step's write. J2's and K2's own `ensureServingRoute` write sits at the top of `runLock`, above every check, so a J2 or K2 `Lock` held by any of the three still follows a promotion; this guarantee is the missing policy's `Lock`'s alone (A79)."

2. **A79 asserts an eight-way mutation-survival claim and runs nothing, in a design whose last two amendments exist because unrun survival claims were wrong.** `03:1390` — "**(a)–(h) all survive it**" — is a claim about **built** tests, over a block move that compiles and a suite that runs in about twenty seconds. `03:1429`'s justification ("A77's mutation table is twenty-three runs still … because it counted what was built") explains the count but not the omission: the survival claim is itself about what was built. The design's own README row records "two of A77's own claims did not hold under measurement, and two of A78's own did not either". *(I considered grading this MAJOR, and the forked critic did. I do not, for three reasons: A79 says plainly "Nothing is run for this row", so the claim is labelled unmeasured rather than passed off as measured, which is the rule-7 discipline this corpus asks for; the row is OWED, so the measurement arrives with the build and the design says so; and the claim is derivable from the code by reading, which I did — `out.foreign` is empty in (a), (b), (d)–(h) and (c) rewinds above both steps — and the forked critic's run confirmed it. The finding is real but it costs a reader nothing.)* **Fix.** One clause at `:1390`: say the survivals are derived from `out.foreign` being empty in every other sub-case and from (c)'s rewind, and are to be measured when the row is built — or, better, run the twenty seconds and add an A79 row to §11's table saying what was run.

3. **The excluded enlarged move fails (e) as well, and §11's own mutation table already says so.** `03:1390`: "The enlarged move, 're-point above the intact read', fails **both** (c) and (i) and so is not this mutation." §11's table at `:1477` records "**Re-point before the stage loop**: (c) killed; (e) also fails, its window having moved." The mechanism is `deleteRouteOnPolicyRead`: (e)'s hook deletes the route on the `<agent>-auth` `Get`, so hoisting the re-point above that read leaves the route present at the write, `errRouteGone` never fires, no `Create` is recorded, and (e)'s "did not become the route re-create's `Create`" assertion fails. The forked critic measured this and it holds. **Fix.** "fails **(c), (e) and (i)**".

4. **(i)'s "Build, in order" gives no reason, and the order that is load-bearing is not the one a reader will infer.** `03:1381` says every sub-case's order is load-bearing, and (c) gives its reason (`authPolicyPresent` matches on name alone). For (i), step (1)→(2) is **not** load-bearing: a foreign policy under its own name never matches `authPolicyPresent`, never trips `foreignAtOwnName`, and the `Lock` reaches `ProbingAfter` whether the policy is planted before or after. What *is* load-bearing is (2) before the **promoting pass** in (3) — plant it after r2 is promoted and any intervening pass re-points the route and the state is gone. **Fix.** Replace "**Build**, in order." with "**Build.** The foreign policy must stand before the pass that promotes r2: a pass that reaches the re-point with r2 carded moves the route and the state is gone. Its position relative to entering the `Lock` does not matter — a policy under its own name never trips `authPolicyPresent` or `foreignAtOwnName`, which is (c)'s constraint and not this one."

5. **`plantForeign` is attributed to the wrong file.** `03:1427` says (i) "needs only this design's own operator and helpers that already exist in `test/envtest/authgate_test.go` — `missingPolicyLock`, `mintRevision(carded)` and the foreign-policy plant the suite already uses". `missingPolicyLock` and `mintRevision` are in `authgate_test.go`; `plantForeign` is at `test/envtest/authabove_test.go:821`. Same package, so buildability survives — the citation does not, and this bullet's whole job is to show the row is cheap to build. **Fix.** "…helpers the suite already has — `missingPolicyLock` and `mintRevision(carded)` in `test/envtest/authgate_test.go`, and `plantForeign` in `test/envtest/authabove_test.go` —".

6. **`docs/designs/README.md`'s design 03 row still reads, present tense, "§8.1 case 18 is written (`test/envtest/authgate_test.go`)".** That clause is A77's and sits well before A79's correction at the end of the same cell, so the row states both "case 18 is written" and "case 18 (i) is added and owed". The Status line has no such problem because it already quotes ADR-0034's "(a)–(h)". `AGENTS.md` tells every reader to trust the README table, which makes the tense the thing to fix rather than to rely on a later clause to correct. **Fix.** "§8.1 case 18 (a)–(h) is written".

7. **The debt A79 creates in merged design 16 is recorded only where design 16's readers will not look.** `docs/designs/16-evalsuite.md:566` says, in a §1.1 table cell of an **approved** slice, "**This row is the only pin on that ordering.**" A79 correctly judges it right as merged and false once (i) is added, and correctly declines to edit design 16 from design 03 (A77's precedent). But the debt is recorded only in A79's §11 bullet and in **design 03's** README row; design 16's own row at `README.md:24` says nothing, and A79's claim that this is "one line on the follow-up inventory A77 opened" does not hold literally — that inventory is the labelled list at `03:1454`, titled "What A77 does to design 16's text", and A79 adds no line to it and opens no list of its own. `reviews/03-a77-critique3.md` MINOR 5 raised this exact class and proposed the remedy: "Either put a line in the README's design-16 row, or say in §9 that the debt is design 03's to chase." *(The forked critic graded this MAJOR. I do not: the corpus graded the identical finding MINOR one amendment ago, the debt was in fact paid that time by design 16 A19, and the sentence at `16:566` is inside an **owed** row that nothing builds today, so no reader can act on it wrongly.)* **Fix.** One clause in `docs/designs/README.md`'s design 16 row quoting `16-evalsuite.md:566` and what makes it false, or a short "What A79 does to design 16's text" sub-list under A79's own bullet, with the verbatim quote and its location.

8. **Two vocabulary and shape nits in §8.1.** (a) `03:1390` opens "**OWED, not built**" where §8.1's own table says "**Owed, not written**" twice (`:1357`, `:1358`); `:49` and `:1381` say "owed and not built". Three spellings of one status in one section is the thing `write-spec` exists to stop — pick one. (b) The range claims inside case 18 now read as "everything else" over a nine-sub-case case: (f)'s "(a)–(e) all survive it" and (h)'s "Mutation 1 is survived by (a)–(g)" are each true as written but silently exclude the new row, and §11's table says a mutation was "run against all eight sub-cases", which was exhaustive when written and is now merely accurate. None is false; each is one word from being unambiguous ("all eight built sub-cases"; "(a)–(g), and (i) when it is built"). **Fix.** Settle on "Owed, not written" throughout, and qualify the three ranges.

## What the amendment gets right, and why the verdict is PASS

Not as a cushion — I tried to break each of these:

- The diagnosis is exact to the line, rests on a code fact that is easy to get wrong (`compiler.Digest` projecting the UID label, which is what makes (c)'s relabel a digest change and therefore an intact-read failure rather than a foreign-check hit), and it is correct.
- The correction to the brief's premise — that `foreignTrafficPolicies` needs only a name of its own, not another Agent's UID — is right, and it is what makes (i) a different state from (c) rather than a restatement of it.
- (i) is not a paper row. Its build reaches the state, its five assertions are each true of the code, its mutation compiles and is killed by two of them, and its survivals hold. The corrected assertion — no exits, because `missingPolicyRouteNote` returns after its first clause on a carded active revision — is the kind of thing a first draft gets wrong and a careful second draft catches, and A79 says so in the open.
- Option A is the right call and is argued on buildability, ownership and present coverage rather than on preference, with the cost ("case 18 was fully built, and after this amendment it is not") stated in both the places a reader checks.
- Marking A77's false clause as provenance rather than rewriting it is the correct treatment of a §11 record, and `:1467` is the last copy of that clause in editable text.
- Leaving ADR-0034 Amendment 6 alone is right, for the reason A79 gives.

Everything left is a sentence. **PASS.**
