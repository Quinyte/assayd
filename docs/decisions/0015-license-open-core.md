# ADR-0015: Apache 2.0 core; open-core by component; never paywall correctness
- **Status**: accepted · 2026-08-20 · **amended 2026-09-10 (Amendment 1: SSO moves to the free core; foundation target is AAIF)**
- **Context**: 2024–26 relicensing wave (HashiCorp BSL, Redis) damaged trust; AGPL taxes adoption; CNCF path requires OSI license. Realistic strip-mining protection at this stage is velocity + enterprise layer, not license.
- **Decision**: Core under Apache 2.0. Enterprise = separate proprietary modules (Tenant CR/hard multi-tenancy fan-out, compliance profiles + audit reporting, SSO/SCIM, fleet management, advanced governance, support). Split line: single team gets the full loop free; organizational scale is paid. Hard rule: evals, receipts, signing, drift stay open — never paywall correctness or safety.
- **Consequences**: Trust-led adoption funnel; enterprise revenue tied to org scale, not crippled core.

## Amendment 1 (2026-09-10) — SSO moves to the free core; the foundation target is AAIF, not CNCF

Decided by the human on `research/licensing-open-core-2026-09.md`.

**The Apache-2.0 decision is validated and unchanged.** Every comparable project checked is Apache-2.0 with no exceptions — cert-manager, Crossplane, Kyverno, Envoy Gateway, SPIFFE, OpenFGA, Flux, Argo, kagent, and agentgateway itself. The relicensing wave this ADR's Context cites settled against itself rather than continuing: Elastic added AGPL back in 2024, Redis reverted to AGPL in 2025 **and still lost** — Valkey holds 83% enterprise adoption — and the durable lesson is that a reversal does not undo the trust damage. Nothing here argues for revisiting the license.

**This ADR contradicted its own hard rule, and the rule wins.** The Decision states "never paywall correctness or safety" and then lists **SSO/SCIM** among the enterprise modules. CISA's Secure-by-Design guidance says single sign-on "should be available by default as part of the base offering" with no SSO tax, and paywalling authentication is now the practice being named rather than a neutral default. **SSO moves to the free core. SCIM, fleet management, hard multi-tenancy, compliance reporting and support stay enterprise.** The split line the Decision states — "single team gets the full loop free; organizational scale is paid" — is unchanged and is in fact better served: SSO is how a single team logs in, SCIM is how an organization provisions hundreds of people. ADR-0026 already models the right shape, with chaining and `compliance verify` free and reporting paid.

**The foundation target is the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), not CNCF Sandbox.** agentgateway — assayd's pinned data plane — was contributed to AAIF, and MCP sits in the same foundation. assayd's two hardest external dependencies are therefore governed together, somewhere assayd is not. AAIF is newer and its process less established than CNCF's, and that is the accepted cost. Note the split in Solo.io's own behaviour: `kagent` and the registry went to CNCF while agentgateway went to AAIF, so this is a real fork in the road rather than a formality.

**Consequences.** Apache-2.0 is a precondition for either venue and is already met. A foundation submission requires a real `MAINTAINERS` file with named people — "TBD" is rejected — plus `GOVERNANCE`, `CODE_OF_CONDUCT`, `SECURITY.md` and a signed contribution agreement, none of which exist yet. **Trademark: a foundation expects to receive a clean mark**, so the professional search ADR-0001 Amendment 1 still owes is upstream of any submission, not parallel to it. **DCO, not CLA** — a CLA's one real advantage is the ability to relicense, which this ADR has foresworn, and the CNCF/LF default recommendation is DCO. Finally, the EU CRA's manufacturer reporting obligations began 2026-09-11; they attach on the day assayd publishes, not later, and publishing under a foundation is one lever for placing the "open-source steward" duties on the foundation rather than on maintainers individually.
