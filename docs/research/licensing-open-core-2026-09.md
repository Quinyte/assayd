# Licensing + open-core practice for a Kubernetes-native infra project publishing in 2026

**Status**: 2026-09-09. Research note for the three open licensing/publish-readiness decisions ahead of assayd going public. **Re-verify by 2026-12-09** (CNCF Sandbox cadence and CRA dates move on their own clocks; the rest is comparatively stable).

**Bottom line up front.** ADR-0015's *license* decision (Apache 2.0 core, open-core by component) is the industry-standard shape and needs no revision — every comparable CNCF-track project checked below is Apache-2.0. **What is missing is not that decision, it is everything a first publish requires to act on it**: the repo has zero of LICENSE, SECURITY.md, CONTRIBUTING.md, CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md, GOVERNANCE.md, MAINTAINERS.md, NOTICE, or a DCO check — checked directly (`ls` at repo root, 2026-09-09), all absent. And the project's own name is not the name it will publish under: `README.md` states "assayd is a neutral internal codename. Renaming before any public release is a tracked task (docs/decisions/0001)" — so trademark clearance cannot even start until that rename happens, which makes the rename the actual blocking step, not a footnote to it.

**Three findings do change something, and the first is a contradiction inside ADR-0015 itself.**

1. **ADR-0015 states the hard rule "never paywall correctness or safety" and then lists SSO/SCIM as an enterprise feature.** SSO is a safety feature by any ordinary reading, so the ADR paywalls something its own rule protects. §2 shows external practice has moved against gating it. This is not a clarification owed; it is an inconsistency to resolve, and the recommended resolution (split SSO from SCIM) preserves the ADR's "single team free, organizational scale paid" thesis intact.
2. **The CRA's first hard deadline is 2026-09-11 — two days after this note**, not three months. It binds *manufacturers*, and an unpublished private repo is not one, so assayd is not late. But the date assayd acquires these duties is the date it publishes, which makes this a publish-readiness gate rather than a future concern.
3. **agentgateway and MCP — assayd's two most load-bearing external contracts — are now governed by the same foundation** (the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation). That materially reduces two risks `docs/architecture.md` §19 currently holds open, and it means AAIF, not CNCF Sandbox, is the natural foundation home.

**Every comparable CNCF-track infra project checked is Apache-2.0, no exceptions.** CNCF's inbound-license default is Apache-2.0; anything else requires a TOC supermajority plus Governing Board vote via a formal license-exception process ([CNCF third-party license policy](https://github.com/cncf/foundation/blob/main/policies-guidance/allowed-third-party-license-policy.md), [CNCF's 2017 Apache-2.0 recommendation](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2017/02/01/cncf-recommends-aslv2/)). This closes the licensing half of the question outright: nothing here argues against ADR-0015.

**agentgateway's governance is the one item worth flagging back into the architecture doc.** assayd's `docs/architecture.md` treats agentgateway as an OSS dependency without naming its steward. It is Apache-2.0 (so the license question is fine) but it is **Linux Foundation / AAIF-governed, not CNCF** — a different foundation than every other binding in the stack (cert-manager, SPIRE, OpenFGA, Gateway API, all CNCF-track). That is a real fact about assayd's dependency graph's governance diversity, not a licensing risk, but it belongs in the risk table (§19) alongside the existing "Rossoctl convergence" row rather than being silently assumed CNCF like everything else.

**The Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF) is bigger than agentgateway alone, and this is the sharper version of the finding.** AAIF is a Linux Foundation directed fund co-founded by **Anthropic, Block, and OpenAI**, with Google, Microsoft, AWS, Cloudflare, and Bloomberg supporting; it launched with 150+ member organizations and is reported as the fastest-growing foundation in LF history. Its anchor projects are **MCP** itself, **goose**, and **AGENTS.md** — and **MCP formally joined AAIF on 2025-12-09**. Projects keep full autonomy over technical direction; the AAIF board handles budget and new-project approval. So the finding is not "agentgateway happens to be Apache-2.0" — it is that **assayd's two most load-bearing external protocol contracts, MCP and agentgateway, are now governed by the same foundation**, one that also anchors the exact standard (MCP) design 03's policy compiler and design 11's Connector CRD are built against. That materially reduces two risks `docs/architecture.md` §19 already holds open ("A2A maturity," "Rossoctl convergence") and it is a real argument for AAIF, not CNCF Sandbox, being assayd's natural foundation home if/when it seeks one — though AAIF's own project-intake process and criteria were not located in primary sources in this pass and need their own check before anyone plans around it.

Also directly relevant: **Solo.io donated its other AI-agent projects (kagent, agentevals) to CNCF while routing agentgateway itself to the Linux Foundation's AAIF instead** — a single vendor split its portfolio across two foundations by project type. And Solo.io's older Gloo OSS gateway was itself donated to CNCF (rebranded **kgateway**), with Gloo OSS reaching end-of-life 2026-12-31 while "Gloo Gateway" continues as Solo.io's commercial product on top of the OSS Istio/Envoy Gateway ecosystem — a live open-core precedent sitting directly adjacent to agentgateway, worth reading before ADR-0015's split line is finalized. **Worth naming directly: `kagent`, the project `docs/architecture.md` §16 benchmarks assayd against, has its own open CNCF Sandbox onboarding issue** ([cncf/sandbox#379](https://github.com/cncf/sandbox/issues/379)) — the competitor assayd's own architecture doc names is already on a foundation path.

## 2. Where the open-core line actually gets drawn

The recurring paid tier, everywhere checked: **SSO/SAML, RBAC/multi-tenancy, audit logging, fleet/multi-cluster management, a hosted control plane, support.** Never core protocol or data-plane behavior. This matches ADR-0015's split almost field-for-field (Tenant CR/hard multi-tenancy, compliance profiles + audit reporting, SSO/SCIM, fleet management, support).

- **Kong**: Apache-2.0 core; RBAC, workspaces, OIDC/SSO, advanced rate limiting, self-hosted Dev Portal, and audit logs are Enterprise-only — described as a "forcing function" where teams upgrade for SSO and audit, not for scale.
- **Tyk**: positions explicitly against Kong's split — MPL-2.0 gateway with "no feature lockout" on the gateway itself; only the Dashboard/Portal layer is paid.
- **Linkerd/Buoyant**: as of 2024-02, the OSS project stopped shipping "stable" release *artifacts* — the code stays Apache-2.0 in the repo, but Buoyant Enterprise for Linkerd is now the maintained "stable" build vehicle (free ≤50 people, $2,000+/mo above). This triggered real community backlash — for paywalling **packaging and maintenance cadence**, not code. The lesson for assayd: the open-core boundary can be violated by *release-process* decisions as easily as by feature flags, and ADR-0015 doesn't yet say anything about release/support-cadence parity between OSS and paid.
- **Cilium/Isovalent (now Cisco)**: core CNI, network policy, Hubble — free, Apache-2.0, no per-node license. Paid "Isovalent Units" gate Tetragon runtime security, egress gateway, load balancer, SIEM export, multi-tenant network isolation.
- **Grafana**: relicensed core (Grafana, Loki, Tempo) from Apache-2.0 to **AGPLv3** in April 2021 specifically to block cloud-vendor strip-mining — a deliberate choice of an OSI-approved copyleft license over SSPL. Enterprise/Cloud stay separately commercial on top.
- **Argo/Akuity**: Argo CD and Flux stay Apache-2.0 and fully functional standalone; Akuity's paid layer is fleet-wide multi-cluster dashboards, a managed control plane, and cross-cluster promotion UX — i.e., paid = operating many, free = running one, the same "single team free, organizational scale paid" shape ADR-0015 already states.
- **Flux/Weaveworks**: Weaveworks, the commercial steward that coined "GitOps," shut down in 2024-02 after an acquisition fell through — a cited cautionary tale about open-core sustainability. Flux survived specifically *because* it was already CNCF Graduated with distributed, multi-vendor maintainership rather than single-company stewardship. This is the strongest argument in this research pass for assayd pursuing CNCF Sandbox status early rather than treating it as a someday-step: single-vendor stewardship is a named failure mode, and foundation homing is the documented mitigation.
- **GitLab's stewardship promise** is the cleanest anti-pattern-avoidance policy found and worth modeling ADR-0015's public-facing statement on: never move a feature from OSS to paid after the fact; simultaneous OSS+paid releases when a feature is planned for both; tests always ship in the OSS edition; no artificial limits in the OSS edition; every lifecycle stage has *some* OSS coverage. ([GitLab stewardship handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/))
- **Zitadel**: frames its AGPL-3.0 + enterprise model as selling *risk transfer* (isolation, uptime, compliance, auditability) rather than gating features — OIDC, SAML, SCIM, MFA, and FIDO2/Passkeys are all in the free self-hosted Community Edition.
- **OpenFGA/Auth0-Okta**: the engine itself is Apache-2.0, CNCF-governed, multi-vendor maintained. Okta's paid layer is a separate hosted SaaS (Auth0 FGA), not a gated OSS feature — the cleanest "SaaS-only, not feature-gated" split found in this pass, and a useful reference for assayd's own AuthzProvider slot (design 24), which already defaults to OpenFGA.

**The "SSO tax" debate is real, named, and directly bears on ADR-0015's line — and it is sharper than "reputational pressure."** CISA's Secure by Design guidance (updated 2026-05-12) states explicitly that products should ship "with additional security features such as multi-factor authentication (MFA), logging, and single sign-on (SSO) available at no extra cost," and that SSO "should be available by default as part of the base offering — consumers should not need to bear an onerous 'SSO tax.'" ([CISA Secure by Design](https://www.cisa.gov/securebydesign) · [pledge](https://www.cisa.gov/securebydesign/pledge)). This has a real naming mechanism behind it, not just a guidance document: the **SSO Wall of Shame** ([sso.tax](https://sso.tax/)) is an established, press-covered list of vendors that gate SSO behind enterprise pricing, and a Grip Security survey of 100+ CISOs found 80% of employee-used SaaS sits outside corporate SSO, with licensing cost cited as the primary reason. It has **not** hardened into anything CNCF-binding, and Kong's 2026 pricing still gates RBAC/SSO despite the CISA pledge, so the practice persists — but the reputational cost of appearing on a named, searchable list is a different and sharper kind of pressure than an unenforced norm.

**This is a real inconsistency inside ADR-0015 itself, not just friction with an external norm.** ADR-0015 states a hard rule — "never paywall correctness or safety" — and then lists **SSO/SCIM** as an enterprise feature in the same sentence as Tenant CR and audit reporting. SSO is a safety feature by any ordinary reading of that rule, so the ADR paywalls something its own text protects. The clean fix preserves everything else about the ADR: **split SSO from SCIM.** Move SSO itself to the free tier (a single team should be able to federate identity without paying — Zitadel's own SAML/SSO live in its free self-hosted CE, for reference) and keep **SCIM/directory-sync, fleet management, and org-scale administration** paid, which preserves "single team free, organizational scale paid" exactly, since a single team does not need SCIM provisioning in the first place. That distinguishes "SSO as basic authentication" (never gate) from "automated multi-org identity administration" (fine to gate), and it is the same shape ADR-0026 already uses for compliance — `assayd compliance verify` runs unlicensed, only the reporting layer is paid — so this is a pattern the ADR corpus already trusts elsewhere. A one-sentence amendment to ADR-0015 closes this before a launch-week critique finds it.

**The rest of ADR-0015's split is squarely conventional, checked against a wider set than gateways alone.** Kong Gateway is genuinely Apache-2.0 and ships several AI plugins free, while withholding token-based rate limiting, semantic caching, PII sanitization, the management GUI, SSO, and MCP/A2A governance behind Enterprise — Kong Workspaces + RBAC *is* its multi-tenancy story, and it is paid. Upbound (Crossplane's commercial steward) keeps its UXP distribution free and fully upstream-conformant, monetizing commercially-licensed provider builds bundled with 12-month support, 6-month security backports, and SBOMs. Kestra's public OSS-vs-paid page draws the same line again: LTS/backports, SBOM-for-compliance, and SLA-backed support are the paid layer, not core functionality. ([Kong Gateway](https://konghq.com/products/kong-gateway) · [UXP 2.0](https://www.upbound.io/blog/uxp-2-0-and-crossplane) · [Kestra](https://kestra.io/docs/oss-vs-paid)) This corroborates rather than contests ADR-0015's shape everywhere except the one SSO line above.

## 3. The BSL/SSPL relicensing wave: where it settled by 2026

**Verdict: not "over," but durably de-normalized as a strategy for a project without an existing dominant install base.** The reversions read as damage control, not vindication, and the durable counter-trend is projects moving *toward* real foundations (CNCF/Linux Foundation) and Apache-2.0/AGPL rather than toward source-available licenses.

- **HashiCorp**: MPL → BSL 1.1 in August 2023, pre-dating the IBM acquisition. IBM closed the $6.4B acquisition 2025-02-27; HashiCorp operations formally folded into IBM 2025-09-01. **Licensing has not reverted** — if anything it tightened commercially: the legacy free-tier HCP Terraform plan hit end-of-life, `terraform import` was restricted to paid tiers (Jan 2026), and HCP Vault Secrets is being sunset entirely (EOL July 2026).
- **OpenTofu** (the MPL fork triggered by the BSL move): a genuine, durable alternative by 2026 — roughly 12% adoption among IaC practitioners (April 2026), 27% more evaluating/expanding, 10M+ GitHub downloads, and technically diverging (native state encryption, provider-defined functions) rather than merely tracking Terraform. Terraform still holds majority share. This is the clearest "a BSL move spawned a durable, foundation-homed competitor" case study in the corpus — directly relevant if assayd is ever tempted toward BSL later: the fork risk is not hypothetical, it has a recent, successful precedent. **It also narrows what "foundation-compatible license" means in practice**: OpenTofu entered **CNCF Sandbox in April 2025 only via a special Governing-Board exception to keep MPL-2.0** rather than relicense to Apache-2.0 — confirming §1's point that Apache-2.0 is the default and anything else is a deliberately rare exception, not a second normal path.
- **Redis**: BSD-3 → SSPL/RSALv2 dual (March 2024) → **added AGPLv3** as a third, OSI-approved option starting with Redis 8 (May 2025); current (Redis 8.8, May 2026) is tri-licensed AGPLv3/SSPLv1/RSALv2, user's choice.
- **Valkey** (the Linux Foundation-governed BSD-3 fork born from the Redis relicensing, launched within 30 days of the SSPL move, ~50 contributing companies in its first year): production-ready in 2026, the default engine on AWS ElastiCache/MemoryDB, steady release cadence (9.1, May 2026), and — the number worth internalizing — reported at **~83% enterprise adoption and roughly 2× Redis's PR rate**. Notably, **Valkey kept that share even after Redis's own AGPL reversion** — the clearest evidence in this pass that relicensing trust damage is not undone by reverting the license text. Redis relicensed, took the damage, reversed, and still lost the community it alienated — which is the strongest available argument for never relicensing in the first place, stronger than ADR-0015's own context line claims.
- **Elastic**: reverted Elasticsearch/Kibana to add AGPLv3 alongside SSPL and the Elastic License, effective Aug–Sept 2024 — the first major reversal in the wave, restoring OSI-approved status.
- **MongoDB**: remains on SSPL; no reversion found, and 2026-specific commentary is thin. Treat as unresolved rather than settled.
- **Sentry's Functional Source License (FSL)**: "BSL, but simpler and time-boxed" — no bespoke additional-use-grant text, automatic conversion to Apache-2.0 or MIT after two years. Adopters beyond Sentry now include GitButler and Liquibase. **Fair Source**'s three-part definition (publicly readable source, minimal-restriction third-party use, a delayed open-source publication date) is a live, named middle path — not dominant, but a real fourth option beyond "Apache-2.0 now" / "BSL forever" / "revert later."
- **OSI**: published the Open Source AI Definition (OSAID) v1.0 in October 2024; 2026 activity is continued regulator engagement plus tooling for the canonical OSI-Approved-Licenses list — not directly load-bearing for assayd unless it ever ships model weights rather than orchestrating them.

**Given all of this, ADR-0015's Apache-2.0 decision needs no revision.** The wave's lesson for a project *going public for the first time* in 2026 (as opposed to a project with an installed base considering a defensive relicense) is unambiguous: start Apache-2.0, stay Apache-2.0, and put the strip-mining defense where ADR-0015 already puts it — velocity and the enterprise layer, not the license. That is exactly the "realistic strip-mining protection at this stage is velocity + enterprise layer, not license" line already in the ADR's Context. This research corroborates that line rather than contesting it.

## 4. What a first publish actually needs in place — concrete, and checked against this repo

**All of the following are currently absent from the repo** (verified `ls` at root, 2026-09-09): `LICENSE`, `SECURITY.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`, `GOVERNANCE.md`, `MAINTAINERS.md`, `NOTICE`, any DCO check config.

- **LICENSE / SPDX headers.** Current convention is **per-file** `SPDX-License-Identifier` (+ `SPDX-FileCopyrightText`) headers, not just a top-level `LICENSE` file — Linux Foundation guidance calls per-file SPDX identifiers "strongly recommended," because SCA/license-scanning tooling is materially more accurate against SPDX identifiers than against pasted license text. The REUSE Specification (FSFE, currently v3.2/3.3) formalizes this as machine-checkable via `reuse lint`. **Adopting per-file SPDX headers now, while `api/ internal/ cmd/ test/ config/ charts/` is still a small tree, is far cheaper than retrofitting it at CNCF-submission time** — worth doing in the same PR that adds the top-level `LICENSE`.
- **DCO vs CLA.** CNCF's IP policy requires one or the other and **explicitly recommends DCO unless there is a strong specific reason for a CLA**. DCO is a per-commit `Signed-off-by:` certification with near-zero contributor friction and no rights transfer; a CLA grants an explicit copyright+patent license, which mainly matters if the project anticipates needing to relicense later (Grafana's AGPL move, e.g., was possible partly because Grafana Labs held CLA-granted rights). EasyCLA is the Linux Foundation's standard tooling for foundation-hosted projects that do choose CLA. **Since assayd is already committed to Apache-2.0 with no stated relicensing need, DCO is the CNCF-aligned default** — enforce with a DCO bot on PRs from day one, since retrofitting sign-off onto an existing commit history is painful.
- **SECURITY.md.** A root-level file (linked from `README.md`), naming: how to report (GitHub Private Vulnerability Reporting is now the preferred channel — a repo Settings toggle), a coordinated-disclosure timeline (commonly a 3-business-day initial acknowledgment, ~90-day resolution/embargo cap), and whether the project will register as a CVE Numbering Authority (CNA) to issue its own CVEs (GitHub supports this directly). OpenSSF Scorecard's `Security-Policy` check specifically greps for this file's presence and quality — its absence is a scored, externally-visible gap the moment assayd is public. **This is the single highest-leverage missing file relative to effort**: it is a page of prose, not a process change, and its absence is checked by automated tooling the day the repo opens.
- **OpenSSF Best Practices Badge.** Three tiers (Passing / Silver / Gold), roughly 180 criteria total. Passing is a realistic first-publish target; Gold is rare (only a handful of projects have ever completed all three tiers) and not expected pre-Sandbox.
- **EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) — dated and real, independently re-verified:**
  - **2026-06-11**: conformity-assessment-body notification rules begin to apply.
  - **2026-09-11**: manufacturer vulnerability/incident reporting obligations to ENISA and national CSIRTs begin — a 24-hour early warning, 72-hour full notification, 14-day final report after a patch ships for an exploited vulnerability, and a 1-month final report for a severe incident.
  - **2027-12-11**: the full regime applies — secure-by-design requirements, conformity assessment, technical documentation, CE marking, full SBOM obligations.
  - **"Open-source software steward" is a distinct, lighter-touch CRA category** — an entity *other than the manufacturer* that supports a CRA-in-scope FOSS product **for commercial purposes** (hosting, administering, or guiding the project's development). Stewards are exempt from CRA fines but carry three obligations: (1) a documented cybersecurity policy covering secure development and vulnerability handling, (2) cooperation with market-surveillance authorities on request, (3) reporting actively-exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents on the same clock as manufacturers. **Whether the steward category attaches to assayd at all turns on whether there is a commercial entity behind it** — this is exactly the open question ADR-0001's "neutral internal codename, rename before public release" note leaves unresolved, and it needs a real answer (is there a company, is there not) before the CRA analysis can be more than conditional. **The September 2026 reporting-obligation date is not "close enough to plan for" — it is 2026-09-11, two days after this note.** (An earlier draft of this line said three months; that was an arithmetic error and it mattered, because it turned a publish-readiness gate into a future concern.) The obligation binds *manufacturers*, and a private, unpublished repository is not placing a product on the EU market, so **assayd is not currently late**. What the date settles is that the duties attach at publish, not at some later maturity — so the cybersecurity policy, the reporting channel, and the named owner are first-publish artifacts, in the same tranche as `LICENSE`.
- **Trademark.** The standard CNCF pattern is that the project name is owned by its original maintainers pre-donation and is formally transferred to the Linux Foundation during onboarding (a Project Contribution Agreement step), after which it falls under LF trademark policy and CNCF brand guidelines. **The practical implication for assayd is sharper than the general advice**: the repo's own README says the current name is explicitly not the launch name, so there is nothing to clear yet — the rename (ADR-0001's tracked task) is the prerequisite step, and trademark search/registration is the step immediately after it, not something that can happen in parallel. No concrete CNCF/LF naming-dispute case study was found despite direct searching; "register early" is best-practice inference here, not a documented cautionary tale — flagged as such rather than overstated.
- **Other first-publish files.** CNCF publishes literal templates (`cncf/project-template`): `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` (CNCF's standard CoC is adoptable as-is), `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `GOVERNANCE.md` (`GOVERNANCE-maintainer.md` is the correct starting template at Sandbox stage, graduating to an elections-based model at Incubation), `MAINTAINERS.md` (a Name / GitHub ID / Company table), `SECURITY_CONTACTS.md`, an embargo policy, and an incident-response doc. `NOTICE` is Apache-2.0-specific and only required if a bundled dependency itself carries one — **checked against `go.mod`: assayd's five direct dependencies are all `k8s.io`/`sigs.k8s.io`, all Apache-2.0, so a `NOTICE` file is not currently forced by anything in the dependency tree**, though it should still be created as an empty placeholder per Apache-2.0 convention.
- **SBOM / supply chain.** The 2026 baseline for a credible new infra project is CI emitting, per release artifact, a CycloneDX or SPDX SBOM plus a SLSA provenance attestation, both signed via Sigstore (Cosign for artifact signing, Fulcio for short-lived OIDC-based certs, Rekor as the transparency log) rather than long-lived GPG keys. **assayd is already most of the way there**: `docs/supply-chain.md` documents keyless cosign signing via GitHub OIDC/Fulcio/Rekor and SBOM attestation for the operator image, and `.github/workflows/release.yml` already does the signing on tag. What is missing is the SBOM/provenance story extending to the Helm chart (`docs/supply-chain.md` states the chart is "not published" yet) and to `test/responder`'s registry push via `hack/e2e.sh` — the same signing path should cover both before either is treated as a release artifact.
- **CNCF Sandbox application, 2026 specifics.** A real `MAINTAINERS.md` with a Name/GitHub-ID/Company table is a hard gate — "N/A" or "TBD" placeholders are rejected. License must be Apache-2.0 (matches ADR-0015) or go through the rare Governing-Board exception process. The TOC must formally vote, and a Contribution Agreement must be signed, before Sandbox status is official. Applications are reviewed roughly every two months, generally oldest-first; no published SLA on time-to-decision exists, but the cadence implies months rather than weeks. **Given Flux's survival-via-foundation-homing precedent in §2, and given that ADR-0015 already anticipates a CNCF path ("no BSL adoption tax"), applying for Sandbox status is worth scheduling as a concrete milestone once the rename lands, rather than an open-ended someday** — the review cadence means a decision late in a cycle costs a full extra cycle.

## 5. What this note recommends, concretely

1. **ADR-0015's license decision stands** — no change to Apache-2.0 or to open-core-by-component.
2. **ADR-0015's split line needs an amendment, not a clarifying sentence.** Move **SSO** to the free tier; keep **SCIM/directory-sync, fleet management and org-scale administration** paid. The ADR's own hard rule ("never paywall correctness or safety") already decides this once SSO is recognized as a safety feature, and CISA's Secure-by-Design guidance — updated 2026-05-12 — says SSO "should be available by default as part of the base offering" with no "onerous SSO tax" ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/securebydesign), [pledge](https://www.cisa.gov/securebydesign/pledge), [sso.tax](https://sso.tax/)). The thesis survives untouched: a single team does not need SCIM provisioning, so "single team free, organizational scale paid" still draws the same revenue line. Note the correct shape already exists elsewhere in the corpus — ADR-0026 keeps hash-chaining and `assayd compliance verify` usable without a license and charges only for the *reporting*. SSO should get that same treatment. For a product whose pitch is "an agent you can trust with your business," appearing on the SSO Wall of Shame in launch week is a disproportionate cost.
3. **A first-publish checklist, ordered by dependency**: (a) execute ADR-0001's rename — nothing else in this list can start without it; (b) trademark search/registration under the new name; (c) `LICENSE` (Apache-2.0) + per-file SPDX headers while the tree is still small; (d) `SECURITY.md` + enable GitHub Private Vulnerability Reporting — highest leverage-per-effort item, do this even before the rename if the repo opens sooner; (e) `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `GOVERNANCE.md`, `MAINTAINERS.md` from CNCF's templates; (f) DCO bot enforcement; (g) extend the existing cosign/SBOM signing path (already real per `docs/supply-chain.md`) to the Helm chart and `test/responder`.
4. **Name an owner for the CRA question now.** The 2026-09-11 reporting-obligation date is two days from today, not three months — an earlier draft of this note miscounted. **assayd's own planned enterprise tier is exactly what makes the "commercial purposes" test for the steward category bite**: a purely volunteer, non-commercial project is closer to exempt, but ADR-0015's own open-core model argues against assayd staying in that category once it publishes. Whether the steward category attaches at all is a factual/legal question this research cannot answer and should be resolved — not deferred — before publish, and publishing under a foundation (AAIF or CNCF) is one lever, since it would put the steward duties on the foundation rather than on assayd's maintainers individually.
5. **Add agentgateway's AAIF/Linux-Foundation governance to `docs/architecture.md` §19's risk table** — it is the one dependency in the stack not on the CNCF track, and the doc currently doesn't say so. **But record it as a risk *reduced*, not merely a difference.** The Agentic AI Foundation is a Linux Foundation directed fund co-founded by Anthropic, Block and OpenAI (with Google, Microsoft, AWS, Cloudflare, Bloomberg supporting), whose anchor projects are **MCP**, goose and AGENTS.md, and it launched with 150+ members — the fastest-growing foundation in LF history ([LF announcement](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-the-formation-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation), [MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2025-12-09-mcp-joins-agentic-ai-foundation/)). So assayd's two most load-bearing external contracts, **MCP and agentgateway, are now neutrally governed under the same roof**. §19 currently holds "A2A maturity" and vendor-convergence as open risks on the assumption of single-vendor stewardship; that assumption no longer holds for the gateway.
6. **Reconsider the foundation venue: AAIF is now the more natural home than CNCF Sandbox**, since both MCP and agentgateway live there. This changes the trademark plan (transfer to LF, not merely register) and determines who carries CRA steward duties — a foundation home puts them on the foundation rather than on an individual or a small company, which is an under-discussed and concrete reason to take the foundation path early rather than late. **Unverified**: AAIF's project-application process and acceptance criteria were not located in primary sources and need their own check before anyone plans around this. Note also that **kagent — the project `docs/architecture.md` §16 benchmarks assayd against — has a CNCF Sandbox onboarding issue** ([cncf/sandbox#379](https://github.com/cncf/sandbox/issues/379)), so the named competitor is already taking a foundation path.
7. **Either way, treat the foundation application as a scheduled milestone tied to the rename**, not an indefinite someday — Flux's Weaveworks-shutdown survival is direct evidence that foundation-homing is the mitigation for the single-vendor-steward risk ADR-0015's enterprise-layer model otherwise carries, and review cadences mean a late application costs a full extra cycle.

**What this note does not settle.** It is desk research against public sources, not a legal opinion — the CRA steward-category question in particular explicitly needs real legal review once there is a commercial entity to evaluate it against, and the trademark section's "register early" advice is best-practice inference rather than a documented precedent (searched for directly, not found).
